# Specialty Home Painting — 10 Things Customers Often Ask

### 1\. Do you just paint, or do you repair surfaces before painting?

**Repair comes first.** Painting over damaged surfaces is usually not the right solution. Depending on the problem, we may use spackle, joint compound, wood filler, putty, sanding, scraping, or other preparation methods to repair the surface before applying paint.

For walls, small drywall imperfections can often be repaired as part of the painting process. Larger damage may require additional assessment and pricing.

**The goal is not simply to put paint over a problem — it is to prepare the surface so the finished result looks right.**

---

### 2\. What makes your door work different from ordinary door painting?

**Doors are one of our specialties.** A door may have scratches, dents, peeling paint, damaged wood, old repairs, or adhesion problems that need to be addressed before painting.

We repair and prepare the surface first, then use **alkyd enamel** when appropriate to achieve a durable, smooth finish.

For exterior and front-entry doors, the preparation is especially important because the door has to withstand weather and repeated use.

---

### 3\. Why do you use alkyd enamel on doors?

**Alkyd enamel is one of our specialty finishes for doors.** It provides a hard, durable finish that is well suited to surfaces that receive frequent handling and wear.

For exterior doors, the finish also needs to withstand exposure to the elements. The specific product and preparation depend on the door and its existing coating.

The important point is that **door refinishing is treated as specialty finish work, not simply wall painting with a different color.**

---

### 4\. Why do door projects sometimes require two visits?

**Because the finish needs time to cure properly.**

Door refinishing generally requires more than one visit. After the appropriate preparation and coating, the enamel needs an **overnight cure between coats**.

The door is therefore treated as a process rather than a same-day paint job. The return visit allows the work to be completed properly, and **rehanging the door and reinstalling the hardware are included in the process.**

We never describe a door restoration as a "few-hour" or same-day job when the finish requires overnight curing.

---

### 5\. How do you get clean, straight lines on detailed trim?

**Careful brushwork and patience are a big part of the job.**

Intricate trim often has shapes and corners that make ordinary taping difficult. Rather than relying entirely on tape, we use controlled brushwork to follow the edge.

On particularly difficult trim, we may use a **one-inch brush and slowly drag the paint along the corner**, keeping the brush under control with the hand positioned on the unpainted side.

If additional stability is needed, a small piece of paper can be used as a moving guide to help keep the bristles from touching the adjacent surface.

It takes more time, but this kind of detailed work is one of the things we specialize in.

---

### 6\. How do you prepare a damaged wall before painting?

First we determine what needs to be repaired.

Depending on the damage, preparation can include **scraping loose material, applying spackle or joint compound, sanding, feathering the repair into the surrounding wall, and preparing the surface for primer and paint.**

Small patches may be included with a room painting project. Damage larger than approximately one inch may require additional assessment, sometimes using customer-provided photos before pricing.

**The objective is to make the repair disappear into the finished wall rather than simply paint over it.**

---

### 7\. Do you paint trim, baseboards, windows and other detailed woodwork?

**Yes. Detailed trim work is one of our specialties.**

This can include:

* Door casing
* Baseboards
* Window trim
* Decorative trim
* Banisters and railings
* Other detailed woodwork

These projects often require careful preparation and controlled brushwork. Corners, profiles, grooves, transitions between colors, and existing damage all require attention.

The work is approached more like **finish craftsmanship** than simply applying paint with a roller.

---

### 8\. Why does preparation sometimes take longer than the actual painting?

Because **the quality of the finished paint job depends heavily on what happens before the paint goes on.**

A damaged door, for example, may require cleaning, scraping, filling scratches and gouges, repairing deteriorated wood, sanding, priming, and addressing peeling or failing paint before the finish coats can be applied.

Likewise, a wall with imperfections may require spackle or joint compound and multiple stages of sanding.

**A smooth final finish often comes from the preparation that you don't see.**

---

### 9\. Do you take small jobs, or do you only do whole-house painting?

**Small and medium specialty projects are an important part of the business.**

You don't have to hire a large painting crew to get professional help with one difficult door, damaged trim, a banister, a room, or a repair-and-paint project.

The business is intentionally structured around **personalized, one-person service**. Issam personally performs the work rather than sending a rotating crew of painters.

Larger projects can also be considered when they are a good fit.

---

### 10\. What is different about working with Specialty Home Painting?

**You work directly with the person doing the work.**

The focus is on **specialty repairs, detailed preparation, doors, windows, trim, banisters, and other projects where patience and attention to detail matter.**

Rather than treating every project as a standard painting job, we look at the condition of the surface and determine what needs to be repaired and prepared before applying the finish.

That makes the business particularly suited to homeowners who have **something that needs to be done carefully rather than simply painted quickly.**
